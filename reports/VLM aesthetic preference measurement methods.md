# Choice proportions settle VLM-versus-human aesthetic comparisons

No study from 2023 to 2026 has run any of this project's five paradigms on a vision-language model, so the progress that helps lies in measurement mathematics, and it supports one rule: **compare model and human effect sizes as choice proportions, and use signs, majorities and raw log-odds only for direction or consistency.** For two options, almost every recent position-debiasing method (PriDe, contextual and batch calibration, CalibraEval) reduces to the 1977 Davidson-Beaver order effect, a constant shift in log-odds that the project's order × letter mean removes exactly, whereas averaging probabilities shrinks a preference by sech²(b/2), which is 0.12 at Gemma 4 E2B's 3.45-logit E2 bias. The readout sets how strong a preference looks: sign and majority statistics amplify a lean, instruction tuning polarises answer probabilities, temperature-1 samples match log-probabilities on average only when replies are independent and the letter comes where it is read, and LLM replications of psychology experiments find model effects two to three times the human size. Classical psychometrics derives the project's curve, P = Φ(μ/√(τ² + s²)), independently, so the literature corroborates `reports/readout_math.md`: the E2 "stronger than people" verdicts (.63 to .77 on the sign statistic) are amplification, and as choice proportions (.514 to .520) the models sit inside the human band. The literature also qualifies that result: a proportion near .5 is also what a near-indifferent responder produces, so the defensible E2 verdict is "no evidence of a stronger-than-human preference"; prediction P6 needs the aggregate correlation for generated stimuli (about −.74) as well as −.87; and τ, the spread of preferences across pairs, is estimable in principle but not at three samples per presentation. Formal aesthetics predicts a homogeneity confound for E1, a null for E2, a dose governed by the ratio of front to back space for E3, a lightness-confounded colour × area interaction for E4, and an evenness effect rather than a golden-ratio effect for E5. The first steps are to audit the hosted replies for seed-coupled sampling, fit a pooled logistic model with order and letter terms to every hosted reply, test whether the local models' bias is additive in log-odds, and replace "matches people" with pre-specified equivalence and scale-free dose tests.

## Benchmarks measure photo taste, and none tests these five paradigms

The 2024-2026 benchmarks of aesthetics in multimodal LLMs score agreement with people on natural photographs and artworks, not sensitivity to controlled composition. On AesBench's assessment items the best model reached **52.86% against 33.33% chance**, with GPT-4V at 50.86% ([AesBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08276v1)). On AesTest's photography subtask, which includes framing choices, the best model scored **0.32, below amateurs (0.35) and far below professional photographers (0.83)**, and the aesthetics-specialised models scored below most general ones ([AesTest](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06360)). Fine-tuning on human scores lifts agreement sharply: Q-Align, which reads a score as the probability-weighted average over level tokens rather than as a sampled word, reaches a Spearman correlation of about 0.8 on AVA ([Q-Align](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17090v1); [Papers with Code](https://ml.paperswithcode.com/paper/q-align-teaching-lmms-for-visual-scoring-via)). Zero-shot, frontier models rank expert-judged artworks at Spearman ρ of only 0.34-0.50, and the plain CLIP-based LAION predictor (0.424) did as well as GPT-4o (0.395) ([FineArtBench/ArtCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09012v2)).

Where benchmarks touch composition, models are weakest. Linear probes of Qwen3-VL and Gemma 3 hidden states recover the AADB attribute **"BalancingElements" at ρ = 0.30-0.33**, rule of thirds at 0.27-0.29 and symmetry at 0.28-0.33, against 0.70-0.73 for the overall aesthetic score; a probe on the vision-only DINOv3 does as well on balance (0.31-0.32), and probe quality does not rise with model size ([Ryu & Yanaka](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11374)). GPT-4o's ratings of rock images track humans at r = 0.84-0.89 on lightness and grain but only 0.61-0.62 on "organized", the dimension closest to composition, and a prompt stressing global organisation did not help ([Dickson et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12178973)). On classic synthetic-stimulus paradigms outside aesthetics no model matched human behaviour: GPT-4V's block-tower stability judgements shared R² = 0.066 with people, against 0.354 for individual humans ([Schulze Buschoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16093v3)). The research found **no 2023-2026 study that re-creates the Assessment of Preference for Balance (APB), Palmer's inward bias, Locher's colour weight or a proportion preference on a VLM**, so the project's design has no direct precedent.

Scale is a weak lever for composition. Qwen2.5-VL went from 7B to 32B with a gain on perception (0.52 → 0.57) but none on photography (0.30 → 0.30) ([AesTest](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06360)), and "thinking" variants aligned worse with expert art rankings (ρ 0.20-0.29) than a plain model (0.50) ([ArtCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09012v2)). Position bias, by contrast, usually shrinks with scale: Llama-2's spread of recall across option letters (RStd) fell from 23.0 at 7B to 4.1 at 70B, and Qwen's order-swap agreement (κ) rose from 28.7 at 14B to 77.5 at 72B ([PriDe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882); [CalibraEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15393)). For Gemma 4 31B and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, cleaner readouts are therefore likely but compositional preferences are not guaranteed, and an E3 inward bias in either would be a new finding rather than an expected effect of scale. Prior-driven answering is a further risk: on altered versions of familiar images, VLMs counted correctly only 17.05% of the time because memorised knowledge overrode the image ([Vo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23941)), which makes "textbook" rule-following plausible, though no study has tested it for composition.

The recurring failures in this literature are the ones the project designed against. GPT-4V "tends to prefer the first row when the two results look comparable", and requiring its verdict to hold in both orders raised its aesthetic agreement from **64.48 to 86.70, beside human experts at 85.00** ([Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09397)). Open-source multimodal models judging image quality in two-alternative forced choice kept their answer under an order swap on only 19-43% of pairs, against 82% for GPT-4V, and some chose one slot 70-80% of the time ([2AFC-LMM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01162)). AesTest and ArtCoT did not control order at all, and AesBench fell back on GPT-based grading when models ignored the answer format ([AesTest](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06360); [ArtCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09012v2); [AesBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08276v1)). Counterbalanced order and letter, a one-letter answer and procedurally generated stimuli, the contamination remedy named by [Hagendorff et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.13988v6), put the project ahead of most published aesthetics benchmarks on design; its open questions are in the analysis.

## Every recent debiasing method is one log-odds offset that the 2x2 design already cancels

PriDe defines selection bias as a model's tendency "to select specific option IDs" and models the observed answer distribution as a prior over IDs times a debiased content term ([Zheng et al., PriDe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882)). It found the option tokens themselves "one primary intrinsic cause": removing option IDs cut MMLU RStd from 5.5 to 1.0, while switching to a/b/c/d labels, debiasing instructions or chain-of-thought did not help. Contextual calibration divides by the prediction for an "[N/A]" input, domain-context calibration by the prediction for random in-domain text, and batch calibration subtracts the mean prediction over the test batch ([Zhou et al., BC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17249)). For two options a multiplicative prior on probabilities is an additive offset on log-odds, so all of these are **the Davidson-Beaver (1977) Bradley-Terry order effect, P(i beats j) = e^λi / (e^λi + e^(λj + γ))**, differing only in the reference input used to estimate γ ([bpcs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11227); [Davidson & Beaver record](https://scilit.com/publications/94078d32740ff4e1fc2839085db1ed84)). With two options and both orders, PriDe's per-item prior equals the project's order-averaged log-odds, and the project's identical pairs are the cleanest reference input in the family, being in-domain and of known zero preference. CalibraEval, the most flexible of these methods (2025), learns a monotone map from **four orderings of a pair (as shown, positions swapped, IDs swapped, and both), which are exactly the project's four cells** ([Li et al., ACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15393)). It beat PriDe on all five judges tested (Llama-3-8B κ 31.1 → 37.4 with PriDe → 39.2) but barely moved GPT-4o, which PriDe slightly hurt.

Under the additive model the 2x2 mean is exact. Writing the reference's log-odds in cell c as ℓ_c = Δ + β(c), with a slot term P and a letter term L, the four cells are Δ + L + P, Δ − L + P, Δ − L − P and Δ + L − P, so their mean is Δ for any P and L, including any position × letter interaction (a derivation in the research notes; no 2023-2026 paper states it). The same cells yield the slot bias (c₁ + c₂ − c₃ − c₄)/4, the letter bias (c₁ + c₄ − c₂ − c₃)/4 and a third contrast, **(c₁ − c₂ + c₃ − c₄)/4, which is identically zero under any bias that depends only on presentation format**; because local log-probabilities carry no sampling noise, its spread across pairs directly measures content × format interaction. If bias depends on content, b(x, y), the mean becomes Δ + [b(R, X) − b(X, R)]/2: the symmetric part cancels however strongly it depends on the pair, but the antisymmetric part is indistinguishable from preference and cannot be removed by counterbalancing, so counterbalanced designs define preference as the order-averaged quantity, exactly as `readout_math.md` does.

Bias in LLM judges is consistently largest where options are close. MT-Bench found that position bias "can almost disappear when the performance of the two models differs a lot", with order consistency of 98.8% for GPT-3.5 against LLaMA-13B but 67.5% for GPT-3.5 against Claude-v1 ([Zheng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)); GPT-4's conflict rate was 5.0% for a lopsided pair and 46.3% for a close one ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)); and a stepwise regression across judges kept the quality gap as a significant driver of position consistency ([Shi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)). Identical pairs are the zero-gap extreme, so the identical-pair bias that decides prediction P1 is likely an upper-range value for the bias on clearly different pairs, and a batch-calibration-style estimate from real pairs, the mean of (c₁ + c₂ − c₃ − c₄)/4, is a free check.

Averaging probabilities instead of log-odds keeps the sign but not the size. With cells σ(Δ + b) and σ(Δ − b), the mean probability has slope **sech²(b/2) relative to the bias-free slope: 0.79 at b = 1, 0.42 at 2, 0.18 at 3 and 0.07 at 4 logits**, the same function as `readout_math.md`'s σ′(b)/σ′(0), and 0.12 at Gemma 4 E2B's median E2 bias of 3.45, as the project found. Log-space averaging is exact only if bias is additive, and psychophysics offers a rival: interval bias in 2AFC is "strong and common" and fits a model in which the observer is sometimes undecided and then guesses with a slot preference ([García-Pérez & Alcalá-Quintana](https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/ab29d99b-ee36-4c49-9d3c-0efa968489e2/full)). Under that mixture, P(first) = (1 − u)σ(Δ) + u·g, and it is **probability averaging that cancels the bias exactly**, not log averaging. The two models predict different plots of each pair's position effect against its estimated preference Δ̂: flat under the additive model, shrinking as |Δ̂| grows under the mixture. Swap-consistency rates cannot separate them, because under a deterministic additive model a pair is consistent exactly when |Δ| > |b|. Human psychophysics already uses the log-space remedy, fitting a separate psychometric function per presentation order rather than pooling proportions ([Ulrich & Vorberg 2009](https://doi.org/10.3758/APP.71.6.1219)), and two-interval forced choice is demonstrably not bias-free ([Yeshurun et al.](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/bias-and-sensitivity-in-two-interval-forced-choice-procedures-tes/)).

Two-image VLM inputs have a mechanistic source of order bias. In question answering over many images, shuffling image order changed about 30% of open-source models' predictions, open-source models favoured later images, and **inter-image causal attention, in which later images attend to earlier ones but not the reverse, was identified as "the primary source"**; a softened attention mask (SoFA) recovered 2-3% accuracy ([Tian et al., CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13792)). That mechanism suggests a side-by-side composite changes the bias rather than removing it, since its patch tokens still reach the language model row by row. The project's pilot, in which E1's direction changed with layout, fits this reading, but no study has compared the two layouts directly.

## Log-probabilities and temperature-1 samples agree only under four conditions

Sampling at temperature T draws from softmax(z/T), so for two letters the sampled log-odds are (z_A − z_B)/T, and **at T = 1 a sampled reply is a Bernoulli draw with the log-prob readout's own probability** ([Meister et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05403v1)). Four conditions have to hold for the hosted and local readouts to estimate the same quantity.

The first is that the sampled letter is produced where the log-probabilities are read. Across six instruction-tuned LLMs, the answer picked by first-token probability differed from the answer written in the text in **9% to over 60% of cases, worst on opinion questions with no correct answer (66.2% for Llama2-7B-Chat, 13.3% for Llama2-70B-Chat)**, and constraining the format reduced but did not remove the gap ([Wang et al., "My Answer is C"](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14499v2)); where the two disagreed, text answers were more robust to perturbation ([Wang et al., COLM 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08382)). Aesthetic preference is an opinion question. The local readout prefills "Answer:" and the hosted readout cannot; the dev probe's 22 bare-letter replies per hosted model (`PLAN.md`) and the local refusal probe's agreement between forced and unaided choices help, but no published mismatch figure transfers to these VLMs.

The second is that the server applies the requested settings. NVIDIA's API reference recommends temperature 1.0, **top_p 0.95 and top_k 64** for Gemma 4 31B and does not document request defaults, seed behaviour or log-probability support ([NVIDIA API reference](https://docs.api.nvidia.com/nim/reference/google-gemma-4-31b-it)). A top_k of 64 is harmless for a two-letter choice, but a silently applied top_p of 0.95 would delete any option below about 5% probability and make counts more extreme than the model's probabilities. The inference backend alone accounts for roughly 39% of out-of-the-box variability across HF, vLLM and Ollama, even under greedy decoding ([Masoudian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04714)), and hosted inference is not bit-deterministic at temperature 0: in one test, 1,000 runs gave 80 distinct completions ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)).

The third is independence, and the project's own runner raises a specific risk. `scripts/run_api.py` sends **seed = pass number on every request in a pass**. OpenAI describes seeds as best-effort ([OpenAI Cookbook](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/reproducible_outputs_with_the_seed_parameter)), and NVIDIA does not say whether its endpoints honour them. If a server seeds each request's random generator from the request seed, as this report understands vLLM-style samplers to do (the notes did not verify this), every request in a pass draws the same random numbers at the same decoding step, and with bare-letter replies pass s would answer A exactly when the letter log-odds exceed one shared logistic threshold θ_s. Each reply would keep the right marginal probability, so P_cells would stay unbiased, but replies within a pass would be perfectly coupled, the effective number of random draws would be the number of passes, and a bootstrap over pairs would understate uncertainty. The replies already collected settle the question: on the presentations the passes share, the share of "A" replies over the 10,000 or more preference presentations in a pass varies between passes only by binomial noise (SD about 0.005) under independence, and jumps by tenths under coupling. The same check bears on the notes' second diagnostic, that with independent passes the probability that all three agree on a presentation is p³ + (1 − p)³.

The fourth is enough samples. The project scores each presentation by the empirical logit log((c + ½)/(k − c + ½)), where c counts replies for one answer over k passes. It is capped at ±log(2k + 1), 1.95 nats at k = 3, and biased toward zero, while the pair "choice" built from its sign amplifies:

| True p per presentation | logit(p) | Expected empirical logit, k = 3 | Expected pair "choice", k = 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.55 | 0.20 | 0.18 | 0.63 |
| 0.60 | 0.41 | 0.37 | 0.75 |
| 0.70 | 0.85 | 0.74 | 0.92 |
| 0.90 | 2.20 | 1.53 | 1.00 |

These are exact calculations from the research notes, assuming the same p in all four presentations. A position bias also costs information: one binary reply carries p(1 − p) about Δ, so when |Δ| is much smaller than |b| the sample size needed for a given precision **grows by cosh²(b/2), ×2.4 at b = 2, ×5.5 at b = 3 and about ×8.4 at Gemma 4 E2B's 3.45**, and with k samples per cell the favoured slot wins every time with probability σ(b)^k, so unanimous cells leave a single pair's Δ unidentified without pooling. Miller's framework for error bars on evaluations gives the matching variance algebra: a next-token probability has zero variance given the item, resampled answers add σ²/K per item, and clustered standard errors on DROP were 3.05 times the naive ones ([Miller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)).

What survives is clear. The pooled proportion C/K per pair, with a Jeffreys Beta(C + ½, K − C + ½) interval ([Brown, Cai & DasGupta](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1009213286)), estimates P_cells without bias, and a logistic model over individual replies, logit P(reference) = μ + u_pair + β_pos·o + β_let·g with u_pair ~ N(0, τ²), estimates μ and both biases. On τ the research notes and `readout_math.md` differ in emphasis and both are right: **τ is identifiable in principle from variation beyond binomial noise across pairs, but at τ ≈ 0.1 against a per-pair binomial noise of about 0.33 on the logit scale with 12 replies, it cannot be estimated in practice**, which is why the project's random-intercept fits ran to 0 or 1. That matters less than it seems, because the share of pairs on each side, P_sign = Φ(μ/τ), needs τ, while P_soft ≈ Φ(μ/√(τ² + 1.70²)) barely depends on τ when τ is far below 1.7, so **the bias-free choice proportion is estimable from samples even though the share of pairs is not.**

Calibration research explains why a model's effect sizes at its native temperature are not human effect sizes. Instruction tuning "polarizes answer distribution regardless of true underlying data uncertainty" ([Cruz et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14614)), and log-probabilities matched human opinion distributions worse than a uniform guess (total variation 0.455-0.550 against 0.363), while distributions the model stated in words did better (0.226-0.291, beside a human baseline of 0.250) ([Meister et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05403v1)). The opposite failure also occurs: after adjusting 43 models' survey answers for letter and order bias, their answers trended toward uniform with "consistently higher entropy than any human population" ([Dominguez-Olmedo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07951)). The local models in this project look like the second case, with mean pair log-odds under 0.15 in almost every headline cell. Soft readouts beat hard ones throughout: soft pairwise probabilities improved comparative assessment ([Liusie et al., EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05894)), and the mean of a judge's distribution over judgements beat its most likely judgement in every setting tested ([Wang, Zhang & Choi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03064)).

## The project's readout curve survives the literature, with four qualifications

Classical psychometrics derives `readout_math.md`'s central result without reference to language models. A choice proportion identifies only the latent difference divided by its noise scale: Thurstone Case V gives P = Φ(Δ/(σ√2)), Bradley-Terry gives P = σ(θ_i − θ_j), and random-utility theory identifies only β/σ ([Train, Ch. 2-3](https://eml.berkeley.edu/books/choice2.html)). For a language model the softmax makes this literal, P_T(A) = σ(L/T), a Bradley-Terry model whose scale is the temperature ([Miller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). Integrating over pairs with the identity ∫Φ(a + bx)φ(x)dx = Φ(a/√(1 + b²)) ([Gaussian integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_integrals_of_Gaussian_functions)) gives Φ(μ/√(τ² + s²)), and the ratio of the sign statistic's probit effect to the proportion's is √(1 + s²/τ²), the same amplification the human-comparison notes derived independently. Zeger, Liang and Albert's conversion of a mixed model's within-cluster coefficient to its population-averaged value, β_M ≈ β_C/√(1 + 0.346σ²), is the same aggregation seen from a mixed model ([Zeger et al. 1988](https://doi.org/10.2307/2531734)). The empirical record fits: across 156 psychology experiments replicated with LLMs, **model effect sizes were "approximately 2-3 times higher" than human ones in Fisher z** and 68-83% of original null results came out significant ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00128v3)); GPT-4-simulated samples "systematically overestimated effect sizes" ([Hewitt et al.](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/large-language-models-can-predict-results-social-science-experiments)); and some LLM replications showed near-zero variation across runs ([Park et al.](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/153319)). The research found no 2023-2026 paper that writes the curve down for LLM readouts, so `readout_math.md` fills a real gap.

| Claim in `readout_math.md` | Literature verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| The four-cell mean of log-odds is the bias-free preference | Corroborated, provided bias is additive or symmetric | PriDe, BC, Davidson-Beaver, CalibraEval; test with (c₁ − c₂ + c₃ − c₄)/4 |
| Averaging probabilities attenuates by σ′(b)/σ′(0) | Corroborated (identical to sech²(b/2)); reversed if bias is a guessing mixture | García-Pérez & Alcalá-Quintana |
| Every readout lies on Φ(μ/√(τ² + s²)) | Corroborated by Thurstone/Bradley-Terry, the Gaussian identity and Zeger-Liang-Albert | Assumes roughly Gaussian pair effects; σ(x) ≈ Φ(x/1.702) within .01 ([Camilli 1994](https://doi.org/10.3102/10769986019003293)) |
| E2 "stronger than people" is sign amplification; P_cells .514-.520 | Corroborated; "matches people" overstates | Effect inflation (Cui et al.); near-uniform answering (Dominguez-Olmedo et al.) |
| Sampled sign statistic ≈ Φ(μ/√(τ² + 1/k)) | Corroborated by the exact calculations in the notes | Requires independent replies; see the seed audit |
| τ, hence P_sign, cannot be recovered from three samples | Corroborated in practice; identifiable in principle | Over-dispersion in beta-binomial or mixed models; P_soft stays estimable |
| P6 cannot separate "textbook" from "noise-free" | Corroborated | Spearman-Brown, noise ceilings, Trott's "number needed to beat" |
| Visibility AUC .75 ↔ d′ ≈ 0.95 | Correct for a 2AFC or yes/no ROC; conservative for same/different | Same/different decision models differ ([sensitivity index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_index)) |

**First, "inside the human band" is weak evidence of human-likeness.** The E2 human anchor is itself a null, with F(1, 19) < 1 in every object set and bars from .48 to .56, and a near-indifferent model, or one whose position bias swamps its preference, also lands at .50-.52. The bias-free P_soft (.496-.532) is inside the band too, so position-bias attenuation does not drive the verdict, but the defensible wording is "no evidence of a stronger-than-human balance preference", with the visibility rule doing the work of showing the model could see the shift. Schröder et al. make the general point: GPT-4 correlated .99 with human moral ratings, yet when items were reworded so their meaning changed, people's ratings moved (original-to-reworded r = .54) while GPT-4's barely did (r = .99), so aggregate agreement does not show the same stimulus property is tracked ([Schröder et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06950v3)).

**Second, P_cells and P_soft answer different questions, and both belong in the report.** P_cells has the form of a human proportion over counterbalanced trials and includes the responder's order bias, as human proportions include people's; P_soft is the model's preference with its bias removed. Their gap, at most .011 on E2 for the local models, measures how much bias hides, and which of the two is exact depends on whether the bias is additive or a guessing mixture, which the local data can test.

**Third, P6 needs the generated-stimulus comparator, and raters differ in kind, not only in noise.** `readout_math.md` derives a single-rater reliability of about .26 from an individual r of about −.44 against an aggregate of −.87, and .14 from Hübner and Fillinger's generated set (−.28 against −.74). The project's stimuli are generated by search, so the closer aggregate benchmark is **−.737**, not −.87 ([Hübner & Fillinger 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4786554/)). Individual human correlations ranged from −.86 to +.39 with an SD of .35-.36 (`docs/experiments.md`), several times the roughly .10 expected from sampling 65 images, so some raters prefer imbalance and the aggregate describes a mixture that no single deterministic responder can represent. Trott's "number needed to beat" turns this into a report format: a single GPT-4 at temperature 0 was matched by the averaged ratings of 1 to 11 humans depending on the norm and correlation measure, and beat about 70-80% of individual raters on two norms ([Trott 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11142632/)). A model correlation reported as a percentile among individual raters, against the aggregate and against the noise ceiling ([Nili et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003553)) is interpretable; one compared with the individual mean alone is not.

**Fourth, the visibility conversion is conservative.** AUC = Φ(d′/√2) holds for a 2AFC or yes/no ROC under equal-variance Gaussian assumptions, but same/different tasks need their own decision models, and because same/different is harder than 2AFC at a given stimulus d′ (a textbook relation the notes did not retrieve), the d′ ≈ 0.95 behind the .75 rule understates how visible the manipulation is to the model.

### A fair common scale

Direction, the ordering of conditions and ratios of latent effects across conditions within one responder need no assumption about noise scales, because temperature cancels from all three. For E3, people chose the inward-facing view .70 of the time at 8% offset and .82 at 25%, a logit ratio of **1.516/0.847 = 1.79** (probit ratio 1.75), and a model's ratio of mean log-odds at the same offsets is directly comparable. Magnitude claims need a matched estimand, P_cells or P_soft against a human proportion, and an interval statement: equivalence holds when the 90% interval of δ = θ_model − θ_human lies inside a margin fixed in advance ([Lakens 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177)), with SE_δ = √(SE_model² + SE_human²) so that human sampling error enters, as in prediction intervals for replications ([Patil, Peng & Leek](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616646366)). A margin of ±.05 to ±.10 in choice probability near .5 is ±0.13 to ±0.25 on the probit scale. With tens of pairs most verdicts will be "undecided": equivalence tests (TOST and HDI-within-ROPE) generally needed more than 250 cases per condition at margins of .2-.3 ([Linde et al. 2023](https://dare.uva.nl/id/403cf67e-6e84-4540-83f3-3f88f7d2c66c)), and the interval Bayes factor's advantage largely disappears once methods are calibrated to the same error rate ([Campbell & Gustafson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07834)). Where the human effect is a range across conditions, as in E2, the honest region is that range widened by the human standard error, which F(1, 19) < 1 bounds at |d| < 0.22 per object set; where it is a direction only, as in E4, the test is one-sided, or a minimum-effect test against Simonsohn's d33 ([Simonsohn 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614567341)).

Stimuli must be a random factor ([Judd, Westfall & Kenny](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028347)). The cluster bootstrap over pairs is valid when stimuli are the only random factor; for claims that also generalise over the four wordings, crossed resampling (pigeonhole or product-weight bootstrap) is mildly conservative, and no exact bootstrap exists for crossed designs ([Owen 2007](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.1111); [Owen & Eckles 2012](https://dx.doi.org/10.1214/12-aoas547)). A mixed-model coefficient is conditional on the pair and must be converted to a population-averaged value before it meets a published proportion. With a handful of models, each model is a fixed effect, and model contrasts should use paired item-level differences, which cut variance by the correlation between models ([Miller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)); Gemma 4 E2B against Gemma 4 31B on identical stimuli is the cleanest within-family scale test available.

## Formal aesthetics predicts weak, conditional human effects for all five experiments

No formal model of aesthetic value has been fitted to these paradigms, but together with the human evidence they give each experiment one signature for a human-like responder and another for a rule-following one, which the analysis can separate.

| Experiment | Human benchmark (project) | Human-like pattern predicted | Signature of rule-following |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 balance (APB) | r across images −.74 (generated) to −.87; individuals −.28 to −.46 | Smooth rise of choice with score difference; plateau below a score of about 20; homogeneity predicts as well as APB | Near-deterministic choice at small differences; no plateau; r beyond the aggregate |
| E2 two objects | .53, a null (bars .48-.56) | Null: people ignore weight and position when framing two objects | A reliable lean toward the geometrically balanced layout |
| E3 facing and position | Inward .70 (8% offset) → .82 (25%); logit ratio 1.79 | Rises with offset, saturating; needs a recognisable front | Ratio near 3.4 (a front/back-space rule), or a centre preference that ignores facing |
| E4 colour weight | Direction only: red > yellow at small and medium areas, absent at the largest | Colour × area interaction; red ≈ blue | Red heavier at every size, growing with area; or blue heaviest (lightness rule) |
| E5 proportion | None for generated grids (55% for real Mondrians, no dose response) | No golden-ratio preference; preference for squarer, more even divisions | Dose response tracking how uneven the altered grid is |

**E1.** APB is the mean of eight half-against-half pixel differences, DCM is the distance of the darkness-weighted centre of mass from the frame centre, and homogeneity is the normalised entropy of the element spread ([Hübner & Fillinger 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4786554/)). On liking, the across-picture correlations were APB −.867, DCM −.844 and **homogeneity +.848**; APB beat DCM for preference because its inner-outer terms partly measure homogeneity, the left-right component carried most weight, and on newly generated pictures the per-person correlations fell to −.28 to −.29. Since then the link has weakened further: rating finished pictures favoured symmetry over balance and balance over proximity while producing compositions favoured proximity ([Hübner 2025](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/0343e5f2-5f99-44a8-8ce4-0d35c6ed9b29)), and APB and DCM tracked perceived balance but not liking for Suprematist compositions people made themselves ([Braun et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12909777)). Translated into forced choice, P(more balanced) = Φ(ρΔ/√(2(1 − ρ²))) for a score difference of Δ standard deviations gives **.58-.64 at Δ = 1 for an individual rater and .79-.89 for the averaged crowd** (derivation in the aesthetics notes). The project's search constraint made APB and DCM correlate .94 on dev, so those two cannot be separated, but homogeneity, mirror symmetry and the horizontal and vertical components can still be computed and entered as competing predictors of each model's scale values.

**E2.** The best direct test of the physical "lever" reading of balance failed outright: participants framed two discs midway between them whatever their size or greyness, and images with the centre of mass on an axis were chosen less often than off-axis versions (42.6%) ([McManus, Stöver & Kim 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485801)). A null for two objects is therefore the theoretically expected human result, and a robust lean toward the geometrically balanced layout signals knowledge of a rule rather than human-like perception.

**E3.** The inward bias is among the most replicated effects in compositional aesthetics ([Palmer, Gardner & Wickens 2008](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/PalmerGardner&Wickens-1.pdf)); averted gaze alone produces it, suggesting a preference for seeing the predicted future action ([Chen, Colombatto & Scholl 2018](https://perception.yale.edu/Brian/demos/Aesthetics-InwardBiasGaze.html)); and people keep **a roughly constant ratio of front to back space across frame widths** ([Forman et al. 2021](https://perception.yale.edu/papers/21-Forman-EtAl-APP.pdf)). In a flip pair the object stays put, so the centre bias cancels and only facing matters. If preference depends on log(front/back) linearly, or quadratically around a preferred ratio, the inward-minus-outward utility is proportional to log((0.5 + d)/(0.5 − d)) for offset d, which predicts a logit ratio of 1.099/0.323 = **3.4 between the 25% and 8% offsets, where people show 1.79** (this report's derivation, on human data digitised from a figure with 17 participants). A model near 3.4 follows the geometric rule more literally than people; one near 1 shows no dose response. Because the effect depends on the object having a front, abstract arrows and wedges should show less inward bias than icons even in people, and titles that change an image's meaning can change or reverse it ([Sammartino & Palmer 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236182916_Aesthetic_Issues_in_Spatial_Composition_Representational_Fit_and_the_Role_of_Semantic_Context)). Reading direction shifts left versus right profile preferences between Arabic, Hebrew and Russian readers ([1999 cross-cultural study](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022022199030001006)), so E3 results should be split by facing direction.

**E4.** Darker colours look heavier, and with brightness controlled red and blue rank heaviest and yellow lightest ([apparent heaviness of colours](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/18325818_The_apparent_heaviness_of_colors)); in Locher's Mondrian-derived grids, **the weight of red and yellow varied with the area they covered** ([Locher et al. 2005](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:666504ef-d049-40d1-a1bc-3f52c1f9cf81)). A linear mass model (weight = darkness × area, as in DCM) cannot produce that interaction; it predicts red heavier than yellow at every size, with a gap that grows with area. The project's colours make lightness a strong confound: by the standard sRGB relative-luminance formula, yellow (255, 223, 0) has luminance about 0.74, red (220, 0, 0) about 0.15, blue (0, 0, 152) about 0.02 and the grey field about 0.80 (this report's arithmetic). Lightness and hue both predict red over yellow, so that contrast cannot discriminate between them, but **red against blue can**: a lightness rule predicts blue heavier, while the human data put blue between red and yellow and not reliably different from either (`docs/experiments.md`, from a reading the project marks unchecked).

**E5.** The largest recent study, with 4,835 participants in 10 countries rating rectangles, found **preference peaked at 1:1 with no peak at 1.618:1 in any country**, and wide rectangles preferred to tall ones ([Lee et al. 2025, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14439v2)); paired comparisons show a weak group-level golden-section effect over strong, stable individual preference functions ([McManus et al., via Frontiers 2016](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00350/full)); and Fechner's own data reportedly ranked the golden rectangle seventh of ten ([Green 1995](https://www.yorku.ca/christo/papers/Green.golden.Perception-1995.pdf)). The project's E5 is not a golden-ratio test but a perturbation of generated grids, and its generator yields a definite prediction. Split proportions are drawn as logistic(N(0, 0.9²)) and each alteration adds ±δ on the logit scale (`src/aesx/grid.py`), which raises the expected squared logit by δ²; before clipping, a single split becomes more uneven about 61% of the time at δ = 0.5 and 71% at δ = 1.0 (this report's arithmetic). An observer who prefers squarer, more even divisions should therefore choose the generated grid G over G1 (shift 0.5) over G2 (shift 1.0) with a dose response, whereas people choosing between real Mondrians and altered copies showed none (F(1, 100) = 0.20; `docs/experiments.md`). E5 is best treated as a test of an evenness preference, not as a null control.

Across all five experiments, the information-theoretic models say what a VLM answering each trial afresh can and cannot reproduce. Order and complexity add to appreciation independently rather than combining as Birkhoff's ratio ([Van Geert & Wagemans 2021](https://elinevg.github.io/publications/2021_PACA_NeatlyOrganized.html)). Brielmann and Dayan set aesthetic value to w_r·log p(stimulus | observer state) + w_V·ΔV, where ΔV is the change produced by learning; fitted to individual raters the model reaches a median trial-level r of .65, and the true trial order explains about 17% more variance than shuffled orders ([Brielmann, Berentelg & Dayan 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10725758)). A VLM answering in a fresh context is that model with w_V = 0, a pure fluency observer whose likelihood is set by its training data: it should prefer what is typical of its training images, such as axial centres of mass and squarish proportions, and it cannot show the sequence effects built into every human anchor.

## Conclusion

Read together, the literature and the project's derivations turn the E2 reversal from a local correction into a general mechanism: scored by sign, a readout without response noise will always look more extreme than a human choice proportion, which is the simplest explanation for the two- to threefold effect inflation reported across LLM replications of psychology experiments. Studies that set model choice rates beside human proportions without stating the noise each readout adds are comparing different quantities, a methodological point this project can make to machine psychology at large rather than only to its own report.

The decisive evidence about VLM aesthetics here now lies in the shape of effects, not their size: whether E3's dose saturates like people's or scales like a geometric rule, whether colour weight interacts with area or simply follows lightness, whether E5 tracks evenness, and whether E2 stays flat. Those patterns do not depend on temperature or position bias, which makes them the comparisons on which Gemma 4 31B and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL can show whether larger models carry human-like compositional structure, a question the small local models, nearly indifferent to composition, could not answer.
